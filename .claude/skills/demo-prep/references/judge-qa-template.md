# Judge Q&A Template

Prepare answers to these questions before the demo. Judges ask some version of these at every hackathon. Unprepared answers lose points; crisp answers win them.

Write your actual answers below each question. Practice saying them out loud — answers that sound good in writing often stumble when spoken.

---

## Category 1: Problem and Market

**Q: Who is this actually for? Who would pay for this?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Name a specific user type and a specific pain. "SMB procurement managers who currently track approvals in email" is stronger than "companies with approval processes."

---

**Q: How is this different from [obvious existing tool]?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Know your comparison. If the obvious competitor is Jira, Slack, or Excel, explain what specific gap you fill that they don't. "Excel doesn't notify approvers in real time" is a real answer. "We're better" is not.

---

**Q: How big is this problem? Do you have any data?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Have one credible number ready — a survey stat, an industry report figure, or a reasonable estimate with clear assumptions. "We estimate X hours per week per team, which at Y teams in the market is Z hours — that's a real cost." Be honest if it's an estimate.

---

## Category 2: Product and Technical

**Q: What happens if the AI gets it wrong?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Have a fallback. "The AI suggests a classification, but the user can override it with one click" is a complete answer. "The AI is very accurate" is not.

---

**Q: How does the AI work? What model/data did you use?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Lead with the user benefit. Then briefly explain the mechanism (one sentence). "We use a language model to classify request urgency based on the description — the user sees the result instantly and can override it." Only mention the model name if it adds credibility.

---

**Q: What did you actually build in 16 hours vs. what's scaffolded/boilerplate?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Be honest. Judges respect honesty and detect evasion. "The auth and DB setup used our starter template; the core request flow, approval logic, and AI integration are all built today" is a strong answer.

---

**Q: Does this actually work, or is it a prototype?**
Your answer:

Guidance: "It works end-to-end — you just saw it" is the ideal answer. If there are known limitations, state them: "The create and approve flow works fully. The analytics dashboard is a prototype." Honesty here builds more trust than overselling.

---

## Category 3: Business and Future

**Q: What would you build next if you had another week?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Name the 1–2 features you cut (P2 stories) and explain their value briefly. This shows you have a product vision beyond the hackathon.

---

**Q: How would you monetize this?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Have a simple model ready. SaaS per-seat, freemium, or enterprise licensing — pick one and explain who pays and why they would. You don't need a full business plan.

---

**Q: What's the biggest risk or challenge in making this real?**
Your answer:

Guidance: Name a real risk (integration with existing tools, enterprise security requirements, change management). Judges respect self-awareness. "I'm not sure yet" is acceptable as a second sentence — not as the full answer.

---

## Handling Unexpected Questions
If a judge asks something you don't know:
1. **Don't bluff.** Judges know their domain.
2. Say: "That's a good question — we didn't have time to fully explore that during the hackathon. Our initial thinking is [X], but we'd want to validate it with [research / users / data]."
3. Move on. Do not dwell.
